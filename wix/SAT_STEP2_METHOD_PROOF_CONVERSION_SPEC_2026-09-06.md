## Proposed Step 2 architecture

### SECTION A — Diagnosis Bridge

Eyebrow:
HOW POINTS ARE ACTUALLY LOST

Heading:
More Practice Is Not Always the Answer

Body:
A missed SAT question can come from different failure points: content, classification, evidence, execution, pacing, or review. The first job is to identify the real source of the miss so practice targets the decision that needs to change.

Support line:
DIAGNOSE BEFORE PRESCRIBE

Design:
- light/cream section
- one strong paragraph, not a wall of copy
- optional six-part diagnostic row: Content / Classification / Evidence / Execution / Pacing / Review
- no unsupported scientific language

### SECTION B — Behavioral Bridge SAT Decision System

Eyebrow:
THE BEHAVIORAL BRIDGE SAT SYSTEM

Heading:
One Decision Process. Applied Across the Test.

Core four-step method:

1. CLASSIFY
Identify what the question is actually asking before solving it.

2. ACTIVATE
Bring up the specific rule, route, or concept that fits that question type.

3. PREDICT
Form a target before getting pulled toward answer-choice traps.

4. PROVE
Use the text, math, grammar rule, or calculation to verify the answer.

Support copy:
The exact local method changes by skill, but the decision sequence stays stable. That gives students a repeatable route instead of a collection of disconnected tricks.

Design:
- four premium cards or a horizontal decision path
- restrained gold for step numbers/arrows only
- avoid oversized iconography
- maintain white/charcoal visual family

### SECTION C — Accuracy Before Timing

Eyebrow:
ACCURACY BEFORE SPEED

Heading:
Timing Comes After the Route Is Reliable

Body:
Speed is useful only when the underlying decisions are correct. We first make the route repeatable, then reduce hesitation, improve recognition, and integrate timed work without sacrificing accuracy.

Three-stage visual:
1. Accurate route
2. Reliable repetition
3. Timed integration

Do not claim that this sequence is universally superior for every student. Present it as the Behavioral Bridge instructional approach.

### SECTION D — Error Repair

Eyebrow:
WHAT HAPPENS AFTER A MISS

Heading:
A Wrong Answer Should Produce a Better Rule

Process:
MISS → IDENTIFY THE DECISION POINT → WRITE THE FIX RULE → MICRO-REDO → HARDER TRANSFER

Body:
The goal is not simply to see the correct answer. The student should understand where the decision broke, state what to do differently next time, redo the problem cleanly, and then verify that the correction transfers to a harder example.

This is a major differentiator and should be visually prominent.

### SECTION F — Student Fit

Eyebrow:
WHO THIS WORKS WELL FOR

Heading:
Built for Students Who Need More Than Another Practice Set

Fit examples:
- students whose scores stall despite substantial practice
- students who know content but lose points through inconsistent decisions
- high-achieving students trying to close smaller error patterns
- students with ADHD, executive-function challenges, autism, or other learning differences who benefit from explicit structure and individualized pacing
- students who need a repeatable way to review mistakes instead of simply checking answers

Boundary copy:
Behavioral Bridge provides tutoring, test preparation, and academic-performance support. It is not therapy or medical treatment.
